Crop Rotation and Soil Health: The Basics Every Grower Should Know

By Muhammad Ali Ashfaq · 2 October 2026

Growing the same crop in the same field every season slowly changes the soil. Nutrients are drawn down in the same pattern, and the diseases and pests that feed on that crop build up. Crop rotation — changing what you grow from season to season — is a simple way to break that cycle.

Why rotation works

  • It breaks disease and pest cycles. Many pathogens survive in soil or crop residue and only attack certain crops. Growing something different starves them.
  • It balances nutrients. Different crops use different amounts of nutrients from different soil depths.
  • It adds nitrogen when legumes are included. Crops such as chickpea, lentil, mung bean or berseem form partnerships with soil bacteria that fix nitrogen, which benefits the crop that follows.
  • It improves soil structure. Deep-rooted and shallow-rooted crops open the soil in different ways and add different kinds of organic matter.
  • It spreads risk. A mix of crops means one bad season for a single crop does not affect the whole farm.

A simple rotation idea

Rotate between crop families. For example, follow a cereal such as wheat with a legume, and then a vegetable or fodder crop, rather than planting cereals back to back. Choose crops that suit your climate, water supply and local market, and ask your local agricultural extension office for rotations that work in your area.

Build soil health alongside rotation

  • Return crop residues and compost to the soil where possible.
  • Avoid leaving soil bare for long periods. Cover crops or mulch protect it from erosion and heat.
  • Reduce unnecessary tillage that breaks down soil structure.
  • Test the soil every few seasons so fertiliser use is based on need.

Key takeaway

Healthy soil is the base of a healthy crop. A thoughtful rotation costs little, helps control disease and pests, and keeps the field productive for years.
