Trichoderma: A Helpful Fungus for Biological Disease Control

By Muhammad Ali Ashfaq · 8 October 2026

Not every fungus harms plants. Species of Trichoderma are common soil fungi that are widely studied and used in agriculture as biological control agents, which means they help reduce plant diseases by natural means.

How Trichoderma helps

  • Competition. It grows quickly and competes with harmful fungi for space and nutrients around the roots.
  • Mycoparasitism. Some strains attach to and feed on other fungi, including certain root-rot pathogens.
  • Antibiosis. It produces compounds that can slow the growth of other microbes.
  • Induced resistance. It can trigger the plant's own defence responses, so the plant is better prepared for attack.
  • Plant growth support. In many cases it also improves root growth and nutrient uptake.

Common ways it is used

  • Seed treatment before sowing, so the fungus is present as the seedling emerges.
  • Soil application mixed with well-decomposed compost or applied to the planting area.
  • Seedling root dip at transplanting.

Getting good results

  • Use a product from a reliable supplier and check the expiry date, because the fungus is a living organism.
  • Do not mix it with fungicides that can kill it unless the product label says it is compatible.
  • Moist, biologically active soil with some organic matter gives it a better chance to establish.
  • Apply it before the disease develops. It works mainly as a preventive tool.

Know the limits

Trichoderma is not a cure for plants that are already badly infected, and results depend on the strain, the soil and the crop. It fits best inside an integrated disease management plan together with resistant varieties, crop rotation and good field hygiene.

Key takeaway

Biological control is about prevention and balance. Used correctly, Trichoderma can reduce disease pressure and support healthier roots.
