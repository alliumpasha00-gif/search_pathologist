Wheat Rusts Explained: Yellow, Brown and Black Rust

By Muhammad Ali Ashfaq · 17 October 2026

Rusts are among the best-known diseases of wheat. They are caused by fungi that produce masses of powdery, rust-coloured spores on the plant. There are three wheat rusts, and each one prefers different conditions.

The three rusts

  • Yellow (stripe) rust is caused by Puccinia striiformis. It forms bright yellow-orange pustules in stripes along the leaf veins. It prefers cool, humid weather.
  • Brown (leaf) rust is caused by Puccinia triticina. It forms scattered orange-brown pustules on the leaf surface and tends to appear in milder temperatures.
  • Black (stem) rust is caused by Puccinia graminis. It forms dark reddish-brown to black pustules mainly on stems and leaf sheaths, and it likes warm conditions.

Why rust is a concern

Rust pustules take water and nutrients from the plant and reduce its ability to photosynthesise. Heavy infection can lead to shrivelled grain and lower yield. Rust spores travel long distances on the wind, so a problem in one area can reach neighbouring fields.

Practical steps

  • Grow resistant or tolerant varieties recommended for your region, and avoid planting one variety across all of your area.
  • Scout early and often. Walk the field regularly during the cool, humid part of the season and check the leaves for the first stripes or pustules.
  • Remove volunteer wheat and green bridges that can carry the fungus between seasons.
  • Time any fungicide sprays with local extension guidance. Spraying early in an outbreak is far more effective than spraying after the crop is heavily infected, and the label must always be followed.
  • Keep records of varieties and disease levels each season so you can see what works.

Key takeaway

Rust is easiest to control when you find it early. Resistant varieties are the foundation, and regular scouting tells you when extra action is needed.
